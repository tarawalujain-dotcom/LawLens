🔒 Understanding Privacy

Note: Privacy laws are different in each country. This page provides general educational information, not legal advice.

What is personal information?

Children have a right to privacy. This includes protection of their privacy, family, home, communications, and reputation.

Personal information is information that can identify you or tell others something about you, such as your name, photos, location, or contact details.

Why does privacy matter?

Privacy helps you have control over information about yourself and decide when it is appropriate to share it.

What can you do?

If you are worried that someone has shared your personal information without your permission, consider talking to a trusted adult or another person who can help you.

Think carefully before sharing personal information online or with others. If you are concerned about how your information is being used, talk to a trusted adult or your school.

Source: UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 16

Source: UNICEF — Convention on the Rights of the Child

🛡️ Bullying & Harassment

Bullying is repeated harmful behavior that can happen through physical actions, words, social behavior, or digital communication.

What can bullying look like?

Bullying can include insults, threats, physical aggression, spreading rumors, deliberately excluding someone, or harmful behavior online.

What can you do?

If you experience bullying, consider talking to a trusted adult, teacher, school counselor, parent, or another person who can help. If it happens online, you can also consider saving evidence and using the platform's reporting tools.

Remember

You do not have to deal with bullying alone. Schools and other responsible adults can help address concerns and support students.

Source: UNESCO — Safe learning environments

⚖️ Fair Treatment

Note: The exact legal protections can differ between countries. This page provides general educational information, not legal advice.

What does fair treatment mean?

Every child has the right to be treated without discrimination. This includes protection regardless of characteristics such as race, religion, language, disability, or family background.

What does this mean at school?

Schools should respect students' rights and provide education on the basis of equal opportunity. School discipline should also respect the child's human dignity.

What can you do?

If you believe you are being treated unfairly at school, consider speaking to a trusted teacher, counselor, parent, or another responsible adult. Explain what happened clearly and ask what support is available.

Source: UNICEF — Convention on the Rights of the Child, Articles 2 & 28

Source: UNESCO — Safe Learning Environments

🗣️ Speaking Up & Getting Help

If something is wrong at school, you have the right to be heard and to seek appropriate help and support.

Who can you talk to?

Consider speaking to a trusted teacher, school counselor, parent, guardian, or another responsible adult.

How can you explain your concern?

Try to explain what happened clearly. If appropriate, keep messages, documents, or other information that may help explain the situation.

Why does speaking up matter?

Children have the right to express their views on matters that affect them, with those views given appropriate consideration according to their age and maturity.

Source: UNICEF — Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 12

🔐 Online Privacy

Privacy means having protection over personal information and aspects of your private life. Online, this can include information such as your photos, location, messages, and contact details.

Why does online privacy matter?

Information shared online can sometimes spread beyond the people you originally intended to share it with. Thinking carefully before sharing personal information can help protect your privacy.

What can you do?

Review your privacy settings, use strong passwords, and be careful about sharing personal information such as your location, phone number, school details, or private photos.

Remember

Children have a right to privacy, including protection of their privacy, family, home, correspondence, and reputation.

Source: UNICEF — Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 16

Source: UNICEF — Convention on the Rights of the Child

💬 Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is bullying that takes place through digital technologies, such as social media, messaging platforms, gaming platforms, or mobile phones.

What can it look like?

It can include repeated harmful messages, threats, spreading rumors, sharing embarrassing content, or deliberately excluding someone from online spaces.

What can you do?

Consider saving evidence, blocking or reporting the account, and telling a trusted adult, teacher, parent, or another person who can help.

Remember

If something online makes you feel unsafe or threatened, you do not have to deal with it alone. Seeking help can be an important first step.

Source: UNESCO — Safe learning environments

📸 Sharing Photos

Photos can contain personal information and can reveal details about someone's identity, location, friends, family, or daily life.

Before sharing a photo

Think about who appears in the photo, who will be able to see it, and whether the people shown are comfortable with it being shared.

Why does permission matter?

Sharing someone's photo without considering their privacy can expose personal information or cause unwanted attention. Respecting other people's privacy is an important part of responsible online behavior.

If a photo of you is shared without your permission

Consider asking for it to be removed, using the platform's reporting tools, and talking to a trusted adult if you need help.

Source: UNICEF — Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 16

🛡️ Staying Safe Online

Staying safe online means protecting your accounts, personal information, devices, and privacy while using digital services.

Protect your accounts

Use strong and unique passwords, keep your passwords private, and use multi-factor authentication when it is available.

Protect your personal information

Be careful when sharing information such as your address, phone number, school details, location, or private photos. Review the privacy settings of the apps and platforms you use.

Be careful with links and messages

Think before clicking unfamiliar links or opening unexpected attachments. If something online seems suspicious, ask a trusted adult or another person who can help.

Keep your devices secure

Keep your devices and apps updated and review which apps have access to information such as your location, camera, microphone, or photos.

Source: UNICEF — Online privacy checklist for parents

🛍️ Buying & Returning

Consumer rights are rules that can protect people when they buy products or services. The exact rights and return policies depend on the country, seller, and type of purchase.

Before buying

Check the price, product information, return conditions, and any important terms before completing a purchase.

If something goes wrong

Keep your receipt, order confirmation, or other proof of purchase. If there is a problem, contact the seller and clearly explain what happened.

Remember

Consumer protection laws differ between countries, so check the rules that apply where you live.

🛍️ Buying & Returning

Note: Consumer protection laws are different in each country. This page provides general educational information, not legal advice.

Before you buy

Before buying a product or service, make sure you understand its price, important terms, and any conditions that apply to the transaction.

What about returns and refunds?

Return and refund rules depend on the country, the type of product or service, and the circumstances of the purchase. Check the seller's return policy and the consumer laws that apply where you live.

If something goes wrong

Keep your receipt, order confirmation, messages, or other information related to the purchase. If you have a problem, you can first contact the seller and explain the issue clearly.

Remember

International consumer-protection guidelines encourage clear information about transactions and transparent processes for cancellation, returns, and refunds.

Source: UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) — United Nations Guidelines for Consumer Protection

📄 Agreements

Note: The legal effect of an agreement can differ depending on the country and circumstances. This page provides general educational information, not legal advice.

What is an agreement?

An agreement is an understanding between people or organizations about what each side will do or provide.

Before you agree

Read the important terms carefully and make sure you understand what you are agreeing to, including any costs, responsibilities, deadlines, or conditions.

Keep a record

When appropriate, keep a copy of the agreement, receipt, messages, or other documents related to it.

If you are unsure

If an agreement is important or confusing, consider asking a trusted adult or qualified legal professional to help you understand it before agreeing.

Source: UNCTAD — United Nations Guidelines for Consumer Protection

🚨 When Something Goes Wrong

Note: Consumer protection and complaint procedures differ between countries. This page provides general educational information, not legal advice.

Start by understanding the problem

Keep any information that may help explain what happened, such as receipts, order confirmations, messages, or photos.

Contact the business

When appropriate, contact the seller or service provider and clearly explain the problem and what you are asking them to do.

If the problem is not resolved

Depending on where you live, you may be able to use a consumer protection authority, a complaints procedure, mediation, alternative dispute resolution, or a court.

Remember

Keep records of your complaint and any responses you receive. The available remedies and procedures depend on the laws that apply to your situation.

Source: UNCTAD — United Nations Guidelines for Consumer Protection

👩‍⚖️ Getting Legal Help

Note: The availability of legal services depends on the country and your situation. This page provides general educational information, not legal advice.

When might you need help?

You may want to seek legal help when you are dealing with a serious dispute, an important agreement, a legal complaint, or a situation where you are unsure about your rights.

Where can you start?

Depending on where you live, you may be able to contact a lawyer, legal aid organization, public legal service, or another qualified source of legal assistance.

What should you prepare?

Write down what happened and keep relevant documents, messages, receipts, or other information that may help explain your situation.

Remember

General information can help you understand a situation, but it does not replace advice from a qualified legal professional.

Source: UNICEF — Convention on the Rights of the Child
